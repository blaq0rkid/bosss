Stop Calling It a Shortage: Why the "Driver Crisis" is Actually a Leadership Failure

"If you treat your workforce like a commodity, don't be surprised when you're out of stock."

I hear it in every boardroom from the Port of Stockton to the Central Valley: "We just can't find enough drivers. There's a shortage."

Respectfully? Nonsense.

There isn't a shortage of CDL holders. There is a shortage of companies that actually know how to lead them. For decades, the logistics industry has operated on a "widget-based" staffing model. You needed a body in a seat, so you called an agency, they sent a body, and you paid the bill.

But as we transition to zero-emission fleets and AI-integrated logistics hubs, the "body in a seat" model is dead. It’s not just about steering anymore—it’s about managing multi-million dollar assets and complex tech environments.

The "Shortage" is a Mirror

When an operation says they can't find talent, what they're usually saying is they can't find someone willing to tolerate a broken culture for stagnant wages. Most HR departments are still using 2010 playbooks for a 2026 reality.

At BOSSS, we don't look for "more bodies." We architect systems. If you're losing drivers, it’s rarely a "market" issue; it’s a Workforce Architecture issue. Are your dispatchers trained in high-pressure communication? Is your management prepared for the nuances of zero-emission logistics? If the answer is "no," no amount of recruiting will save you.
